# Archer collapse art review

Built-in image generation produced five candidates. The accepted original is `source-row.png` (candidate 4), with successive prompts preserved in `prompt-v1.txt` through `prompt-v5.txt`. Candidate 1 had the final bow touching the canvas boundary and switched the apparent bow hand in the lying poses. Candidate 2 retained the wrong-hand geometry. Candidate 3 redrew the last poses so the right hand supports the cheek and the left arm crosses from the far shoulder to the bow. Independent review rejected candidate 3 because the left hand moved to the bow tip instead of maintaining its central grip. Candidate 4 corrects this: the left elbow bends from the far shoulder, the left palm holds the central leather-wrapped grip, the two bow limbs extend on opposite sides, and a taut string joins the tips. Candidate 5 attempted extra margins but introduced extensive near-transparent background noise and was rejected. The accepted candidate 4 already has a complete bow tip with 9 pixels of opaque-bound right margin. These corrections were generated, not painted over by extraction.

Six distinct authored poses: buckle, one-knee drop, both-knee support, side collapse, ground contact, settled side-prone. The final silhouette is lower than the preceding one. Hood, quiver and unloaded bow remain present throughout. No gore or effects. The same anatomical left hand retains the central grip through the collapse. At native size both facings read consistently; fine bowstring and hand details cannot remain individually legible at this display size.

Common presentation scale is 0.096. It was selected against the accepted idle frame at scale 0.072 using matching hood/body size, not independently normalized frame heights. No pose is resized, rotated, retouched or re-painted in the atlas. Source support anchors use y=584 for the first four poses and y=604 for the final two, reflecting their foreground bow/contact footprint; this keeps the grounded bow near the same displayed baseline without artificially sliding the prone figure downward. The x anchors shift from the original stance toward hip/leg support so the head progressively drops and moves forward. All pose geometry remains at the source scale.

Playback is 60/70/90/110/110/180 ms (620 ms), followed by the runtime integration's 160 ms after-clip fade. The GIF is a single-play offline art diagnostic, not captured game footage: idle 350 ms, six collapse frames 620 ms, then the final lying frame held an additional 350 ms. It has no return to idle and no loop extension.

`extract.py` extracts six alpha-connected bodies with a three-pixel perimeter, retaining exact original RGBA for every kept pixel. It discards only detached low-alpha generation noise: 8,986 pixels, maximum alpha 13/255. `verify-extraction.py` independently rebuilds and compares the atlas byte-for-byte at the pixel level, with per-frame pixel hashes and source/runtime PNG hashes in `extraction-review.json`. Passed locally. The atlas is 2111x429; source is 2172x724. The last pose has 9px opaque-bound margin on the right and is not clipped.

Native-size and 3x registration sheets include accepted idle, six collapse frames, and original archer base frame, with mirrored lower rows. This is an art-only delivery; no repository code, manifest or commit was changed.
