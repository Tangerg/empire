## Visual review

Viewed all five complete sources, the selected native strips and 2× nearest-neighbor strips, original native strips, and all four actual-adjacency 8×8 connected grids on black, white and olive. Grid NESW bits are derived from actual occupied neighbors, not arbitrary mask tiling. Diagnostic enlargement is not an HD deliverable.

- **Reed bank:** Broad olive leaves and large pale seed heads remain visible in the tiny central core and north/west arms. Unlike corner-only plants, the subject survives masking. Wet ochre and muted blue shallow-water patches remain visible in the other arms. The full source has more leaf divisions and more puddle/earth shade shapes than requested, and smooth large-scale shading, but no granular brush grain or speckled microtexture. The same prominent tuft repeats at every junction; leaf shapes are cut at some cardinal endpoints. Much of the whole shoreline context is inevitably lost. The result reads as a reedy wet ground network, not a naturally irregular shoreline or a botanical close-up.
- **Shoal:** The correction removes the camouflage-like water patches and excess thin ripple lines. It produces three broad tan sand areas rather than the requested two, plus two pale-blue current ribbons, while preserving three large warm-gray stones. Smooth broad gradients and additional large stone facets remain; no granular texture remains. The central and east/south stones are clearly visible at native32. Repeated central stones and endpoint stone fragments make the connected grid conspicuously regular, more like stone-dotted shallow water than a wide natural shoal. Sand context is partly clipped, and a prominent stone at every core can look like a stepping-stone pattern. This is a material limitation, not a traversability or gameplay change.
- **Deep-water rapids:** Dark blue troughs strongly distinguish the material from the pale shoal; connected arms retain broad pale diagonal current bands. The isolated mask's tiny center is mainly dark water, so the strongest rapid cue is in connected arms. The full source has two especially dominant diagonal bands with more tapered secondary blue strokes than the requested simple three-ribbon design, but these are large clean color shapes rather than foam speckles or fine filigree. Source water is brighter and bluer than the original subdued atlas. A single fixed diagonal motif repeats through turns and T-junctions, so it cannot express physically consistent flow in every topology.
- **Pontoon board/edge material:** Broad warm timber boards cover all cardinal endpoints and central core; dark joints and a thicker reinforcing strip survive. Rope lashing reduces to a tiny central-edge accent and cannot carry all of the full source's construction detail. The source has six wide boards, broad shaded faces, restrained bevel outlines and a few large edge notches, without wood-grain microtexture. No water or complete bridge object was generated. The connected grid reads as a timber deck network with regular ladder-like repetition. Board orientation stays fixed across all connector shapes, and seam phases do not establish a bespoke structural solution at each turn.

All grids preserve the historical small rounded core and narrow orthogonal arms. Exact edge-pixel equality is not a claim of seamless interior motifs, natural shoreline shape, directional fluid simulation or full live-scene acceptance. No mask was broadened to conceal loss of context and no extra source variant was invented.
